Optical Color-Dot Storage: Principles & Science

How self-calibrating CIELAB swatches and reserved Black & White markers solve physical paper storage
🎯

1. Why Black & White are Reserved

Pure Black ($K=100\%$) and Paper White provide the highest possible contrast on paper ($L^* \approx 0$ vs $L^* \approx 95$). By reserving black and white strictly for finder patterns, timing tracks, and reference bounds, the computer vision engine can detect orientation and unwarp perspective even under low-light or angled smartphone photography.

🎨

2. Self-Calibrating Reference Swatches

Printers print using subtractive CMYK inks, while cameras capture additive sRGB light. Ambient room lighting (warm 2700K incandescent vs cold 6500K daylight) heavily distorts colors. By printing the exact reference palette right on the sheet's border, the decoder measures how that specific printer and lighting distorted each color, enabling accurate relative classification!

🔬

3. CIELAB & CIEDE2000 Color Space

Unlike raw RGB (which is non-linear and device-dependent), CIELAB ($L^*, a^*, b^*$) models human and optical perception. CIEDE2000 ($\Delta E_{00}$) calculates perceptual difference accurately, ensuring that color matching remains robust even when printer colors vary in saturation or brightness.

🛡️

4. Reed-Solomon Error Resilience

Even if paper gets smudged, ink bleeds slightly, or a dot is misread due to a camera glare reflection, Reed-Solomon ECC over Galois Field $GF(2^8)$ automatically detects and corrects corrupted symbols, guaranteeing 100% data fidelity.
